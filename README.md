# Wealth Revolution Fact Find

A Fact Find you fill in on screen and save as a PDF. Later steps add live capture from Teams calls.

## Use it

Open `app/index.html` in Microsoft Edge or Google Chrome. There is nothing to install.

- **Fill in** each section from the list on the left, in this order: Client info, Occupation, Income & household, Existing cover & retirement, Investments, Medical, will & short-term, Debts, Children, Health. The advisor sections are at the bottom of the list. The PDF and the voice "move on" follow the same order.
- **Save** keeps the client's answers as a `.json` file. **Open…** reopens one. Your work is also kept as a draft in the browser, so a closed tab does not lose it.
- **Preview PDF** and **Save PDF** make the finished Fact Find in the Wealth Revolution layout. Lists such as debts and children continue onto extra pages as needed. Every Notes box (and the Comments and list-row notes) is printed in full in its own section, however long. The PDF's font has no shape for some symbols, so they print as plain text: "…" as three dots, a bullet as a dash, "→" as "->", "≥" as ">=", "≈" as "~"; curly quotes and long dashes are straightened, and accented letters are kept. Anything else without a shape (a tick, an emoji) prints as "?".
- **The top bar is in three groups.** On the left **Settings**, **New** and **Open…**; in the middle the meeting timer, **Voice** and **Listen**; on the right **Save**, **Save as…**, **Preview PDF** and **Save PDF**. The buttons have a row of their own under the title, so they stay in the same places on any screen width; on a narrow screen the three groups stack.
- **The Shortfall quote needs a group.** When **Group risk through employer** (Existing cover & retirement) is **No** or **Not sure**, the Shortfall quote part of Personal risk quotes does not show its boxes. It says "No Group exists for a Shortfall Quote to be compiled" (with where to change it), on the screen and in the PDF, and the voice mode skips those boxes. Nothing is deleted: set it back to Yes, or leave it unanswered, and the boxes return with what was in them.
- **Children: name and surname apart, and the father's surname.** Each child has **Name** and **Surname** boxes. The client now has a **Gender** box (Client info, after the ID number) that fills itself, while empty, from a valid South African ID number (the four digits after the date of birth: 5000 and over is male); it can be set by hand or by voice ("male", "female"). When the client is **male**, his surname goes into each child's Surname box by itself: for a new child, when the gender or surname is set later, and when his surname is corrected (a child still carrying the old one follows; a surname typed for a child is never touched). A female client's children are left empty. "Anna Kuiper" typed in a child's Name box with the surname already there, or said aloud, goes to Name and Surname; two first names ("Anna Maria") stay together unless the last word is the father's surname. A Fact Find saved earlier with one "Name & surname" box is opened with the names split (the last word is the surname; "van", "de", "der" and the like stay with it, and the client's surname is split off when the name ends with it), and the saved file now has a `sname` for each child, which anything reading the file (the summary builders) will see.
- **An ID number can be on the Fact Find once.** The client's ID or passport number and each partner's or shareholder's ID cannot repeat (spaces, dashes and capitals are ignored, so "721209 5268086" is "7212095268086"). Typed again anywhere else, the box goes back to what it held and a message says where the number already is ("already used for the client", or "for partner / shareholder 2 (name)"); said aloud, the number is not filled, a word appears under the box and the saved log says why. A file that already repeats a number (older, or edited) is not changed but is flagged on both boxes and in Checks. The client who is also a shareholder therefore has no ID in the shareholder row: the number is already on the form.
- **A child's age is worked out beside the date of birth.** Each child has an **Age** box between Date of birth and Education stage: whole years on the date of the form (**Date completed**, not today), or months under a year ("17 years", "1 year", "5 months"), empty without a date of birth. It cannot be typed over and prints in the PDF next to the date of birth. The six boxes of a child sit on one row when the card is wide enough and wrap when it is not. The **Education stage** still follows the date of birth until it is set by hand; its calculated wording used to differ from the dropdown ("High school" against "High School", "Not yet in school" against "Not Yet in School"), which left the box blank, and now matches.
- **Risk calculation starts with defaults** (shown as "preset", and changeable): in **Life cover: fractured cover** ACI Fixed, ACI value ?, Life cover (WOL) ABI 3%, Childcare ABI 5%; in **Permanent disability: fractured cover** ACI Fixed, ACI value ?, Childcare ABI 5%; in **Dread disease** ACI Fixed, ACI value ?, ABI 3%; in **Income protection / sickness benefit** ACI Fixed, ACI value ?, ABI 5%. The "?" is typed in as a placeholder for the value still to be found. The standard cover boxes have no defaults. A new Fact Find starts with them; a Fact Find saved earlier keeps what it had.
- **The income protection insured amount is the take-home net income.** In Risk calculation, **Insured amount (R p.m.)** under Income protection / sickness benefit is worked out from **Take-home net income (R p.m.)** on Income & household, always, whether that was typed or said, and follows when it changes. It cannot be typed over, and an amount typed in an older file is ignored. It prints in the PDF. Empty until the take-home income is entered.
- **Direct Net Income** is calculated from the gross income using the SARS tables. It is gross less PAYE and UIF, the same as TaxTim's "Take-home pay".
- **Date of birth and age.** A South African ID number holds the date of birth, so when the ID number is typed or said the date of birth fills itself in. It takes the status of the ID number: amber "verify" while the ID is, and "confirmed" as soon as the ID is (typed, clicked, or agreed to by the client), because the date comes straight from the ID. The date of birth always follows the ID number: when the ID is entered or changed (typed, or said), the date is changed to match it straight away, even over a different date entered earlier, and a note says so. A date typed or said after the ID is left as it is, and the Checks warn when it does not match. This is worked out on this computer; the ID number is never sent to Claude. A date already typed or said is left alone, and the Checks warn when it does not match the ID. If the ID number is later corrected, a date that came from the old number follows it. **Age at date of form** (next to the date of birth) is worked out from the date of birth and the Date completed, so it changes with either. It is printed on the PDF too.
- **Length indicator.** Under the ID number and the income tax number a small tag counts the digits as they are typed or said: grey when empty ("13 digits", "10 digits"), amber while short ("7 of 10 digits"), green when right ("13 digits ✓"), red when there are too many, a letter is in it, or the ID's check digit or date does not fit. A South African ID number has 13 digits and an income tax number 10. For a passport there is no tag. The Checks on the right warn too. Nothing is blocked.
- **Verify to confirmed.** A box filled by voice shows an amber "verify" (or "heard") tag. Click the tag to confirm it, or, in voice mode, let the client agree: when a value is read back or asked about ("so that's John Snow, is that right?") and the client says yes, Claude confirms every box that was covered, and the tag turns to "confirmed" with a green flash. A bare "yes" confirms the box being asked about. A number read back that is not the one in a private box (ID, tax, phone) is not taken as agreement, "okay" or "mm hm" confirms nothing, and "no" with a new value is a correction. "Scratch that" undoes a confirmation. Claude is told only which boxes are waiting, never the ID, tax, phone or email values.
- **Checks** on the right warn about an invalid ID number, a date of birth that does not match the ID, a bad email or phone number, and duties that do not add to 100%. Nothing is blocked.

## Look and colours

The page uses the Wealth Revolution logo (`app/assets/logo.png`, shown in the top bar and as the browser-tab icon) and colours taken from it: the logo blue `#27a8e0` for lines, stripes and focus rings, a deeper blue of the same hue (`#0f6fa3`) for text, buttons and the open section, and the logo's charcoal grey for the advisor-only pages. Amber still means "to verify", green "confirmed", and the red glow is the box Claude is waiting for.
All colours live as variables at the top of `app/css/app.css`, with a light and a dark set, so a colour is changed in one place. The PDF uses the same blue (see the top of `app/js/pdf.js`).

## Listening (the microphone)

**Listen: Off / On** in the top bar turns the microphone into the words voice mode reads (it switches Voice on too). Each line that is heard goes through exactly the same path as a typed line. A line under the top bar shows what is going on and the last thing heard, with a small meter for how loud the microphone is.

Three speech sources, chosen in Settings:
- **On this computer** (the default). The sound never leaves the computer. A speech model is downloaded once (Moonshine Tiny roughly 50 to 100 MB, Whisper Tiny about 40 MB, the Base and Small ones more) from Hugging Face by way of the jsDelivr CDN, and kept in the browser (IndexedDB), so it needs the internet only the first time. The model is chosen in Settings (Speech model). **Moonshine Tiny is the default**: Whisper always reads a full 30 seconds of sound however short the line, which took 5 to 6 seconds a line on the laptop without a graphics chip, while Moonshine reads only as much sound as there is, so a short answer comes back in a fraction of that. If Moonshine cannot start (a file that is not published, say), Whisper Tiny is used instead and the line under the top bar says so. The model runs in a background worker (so the page and the microphone never freeze while a line is being read; the worker is built from text in `listen.js` because a page opened from a file cannot start a worker from another file, and it asks the page to keep the model's files). It uses the graphics chip when Chrome offers one that really works (WebGPU, checked for a graphics adapter first) and the processor otherwise (the line under the top bar says which, and why); a graphics chip that fails is dropped at once, remembered, and the processor takes over in a fresh worker. If a worker cannot be started at all, the model runs on the page itself. Each line is cut at a pause of about 0.6 s, first brought up to a normal level (a built-in laptop microphone is often very quiet, and the models make words up when they hear too little), the number of words it may write is limited to what the line's length allows, and what the model writes for plain noise ("[BLANK_AUDIO]", "Thank you.", or the same few words over and over) is dropped, with a note saying the line was only noise and how loud it was. The loudness meter is on a decibel scale, so quiet speech still moves it. After each line the strip shows how long the speech was, how long reading it took, and how long the form then took to update, so every step of the delay can be seen.
- **Why the processor is used.** When the line under the top bar says "on the processor", the part in brackets says what Chrome reported about the graphics chip (no WebGPU on the page, WebGPU but no graphics adapter, or an error), so a slow laptop can be diagnosed from one screenshot.
- **Live Client Conversation.** A text box in the right-hand pane, under Checks (it stays in view while the form scrolls; the version and tax tables sit small under the heading at the top left). It is there from the start and says to press Voice. Every line heard goes in, oldest at the top and the latest at the bottom, and the box stays on the latest line unless you scroll it back to read. Under each line it says what was done on the form because of it ("filled Surname", "took back Cell number", "pointed at ID number") and how long each step took. A line is labelled Planner or Client only when the source says who spoke (the hidden test line with "me:" or "client:"); one microphone cannot tell the two apart, and the client's side of a Teams call is not captured yet. The box shows the last 200 lines; the saved log keeps the whole session (up to 5,000 lines) until the page is reloaded. **Save log** (top right of the box) saves a file of every line: what was heard, what Claude was shown and answered (with the lines before it), what the form did, and the timings. Numbers and email addresses are hidden in it (as [number] and [email], and as [N1] in what Claude was shown) unless the Settings switch lets Claude see them. It is for finding a problem: send the file along with the report.
- **A read-back is a question.** "Your ID number is 7212095268086, is that correct?" or "can you confirm your tax number" never confirms the box, even when it already holds that number: it only points at it, and it turns green when the client says yes. The words decide, because Chrome's recognition writes no question marks: "I just want to confirm you said R150 000, correct?", "you said ... is that right" and a line of five words or more that ends in "correct" or "right" without opening with an agreement ("so your wife's cell number is ... correct") are read-backs too, and the instructions to Claude say the same. The same value said again for a box (Claude repeating it for another box, or the adviser echoing what the client just said, "950,000. Perfect.") is left alone: it never turns a box green. Only the client's yes does.
- **Undo, clear and "empty this box" need the words.** Claude's "undo" is only done when the line says so ("scratch that", "undo", "never mind"), and "clear" or "empty" only when it says clear, delete, remove, erase or scratch. In the first recorded conversation a stray "undo" removed the Occupation and a "clear" on "is definite errors" wiped a name. One box named in the line ("delete his name", "remove the occupation") is emptied with Claude's `empty` action (an empty value for a box means the same); "scratch that" brings it back. The page's own guess that notes begin ("in the notes", "scratch all notes") is no longer trusted: those lines go to Claude, which starts notes only when someone wants to take notes.
- **An email address said in pieces is waited for.** While a personal or work email box is waiting, or one is named ("my work email address is Paul k"), or the line before said an address is coming ("my personal email address is"), short pieces ("it's pool one", "dot kafentzis", "at gmail dot com", spelled letters "K A F E N T Z I S") are kept on this computer, never sent to Claude, until the address ends in a domain (.com, .co.za ...). It is then read as one line, so Claude only ever sees the marker [E1]. "At", "dot" and spelled letters are part of the address, never filler; spoken digits before the "at" are digits ("paul k one" is paulk1); spaces are taken out ("Paul k at life risk dot co dot za" is paulk@liferisk.co.za). A whole address said or spelled in one line is written plainly first, so it is hidden from Claude the same way. "Let's try that again" drops what was kept; a line that is not a piece of an address ends it, and when what was heard already looks like an address it is put in the box, amber, so nothing said is lost. Because Claude never sees an address it cannot correct a misheard name in it, so the page does: a word of the address that sounds like the first name or surname already on the form ("cafences" for Kafentzis) is written as the form has it, and the log line says so.
- **"The email address is incorrect" and "delete that" empty the email box, at once.** They used to be taken for pieces of an address and kept (the box was never emptied and Claude never saw them). Now a short line with a delete word (delete, remove, clear, erase, wipe) and "that", "it" or "the personal/work email address", or a line saying the named email address is incorrect or wrong, drops whatever pieces were kept and empties the box (the page does it itself, with no wait for Claude; "scratch that" brings the address back), and the next pieces start a new address. "Scratch that" and "never mind" are requests and never start an address. A line with more in it ("I will remove it from my mailing list later") is not taken as a request. A misheard surname that begins with an extra vowel ("offenses" for Kafentzis) is matched to the form as well. Lead-in words such as "addresses" are no longer kept in the address.
- **A misheard address is repaired, not just kept.** Chrome's recognition writes names, letters and domains badly ("heaul1.aafem" for paul1.kafentzis, "with life rest.co.za" for "at liferisk.co.za"), and Claude never sees an address, so the page repairs it with what it knows: a word of the address that is a letter or two off the first name or the surname on the form is written as the form has it (a name with something added, such as "paulk" or "pkafentzis", is left alone), a domain a letter or two off a common one (gmail.com, outlook.com, mweb.co.za ...) or the firm's own (liferisk.co.za) is written as that one, and "with" or "add" heard for "at" is read as "at" when a domain follows. What was changed is said in the conversation box and the box stays amber. The list is `EMAIL_DOMAINS` in `js/ui.js`.
- **"Not" or "no" among the pieces of an address no longer turns it into "None".** The email boxes have a "No personal email" tick, and the page's general reading of an answer took the word "not" in "paul one, not defenses, at gmail.com" as "I have no email address", so the pieces were held for ever (found in the 14:40 log). Addresses are now read with `vmEmailParse`, which only joins the words into an address. "I need to correct that" (and "fix that", "redo it") also drops a number or address being held.
- **What Chrome's recogniser also thought it heard goes to Claude.** Chrome returns up to three wordings for each line; the best one was used and the others thrown away. In the 26 October log Chrome gave "my surname is confesses" at 92% sure (the other wordings were "compensus" and "confessis") and "we are not the broken record" ("Brokers on record" was one of the others). Now up to two other wordings are sent with the line as `heard_alternatives` (numbers and addresses hidden as usual), and Claude's instructions say to use them, to spell a name as it already is on the form ("confesses", "offenses" for Kafentzis), and list the South African mishearings seen so far ("pt1 limited" for (Pty) Ltd, "chair holding", "University" for annuity, "text number"). Chrome's own confidence does not tell a right line from a wrong one (mean 0.89), and "Kafentzis" never appeared among its wordings in 50 minutes: for names, a speech service that can be given the names is the real fix.
- **Undo and clear are never done on a guess.** With Claude available, "scratch that" or "cancel that" goes to Claude, which sees the lines before it; and on a read-back ("... [a number] correct") nothing is undone or cleared, because Chrome heard "can you confirm that" as "cancel that" and the cell number was removed.
- **Pause** (top right of the Live Client Conversation box, while voice is on) stops anything said being read, for a remark to yourself or a side conversation: the lines still show in the box, marked "paused: not read". **Resume** reads again.
- **A number said slowly, in pieces, is waited for.** While an ID, tax or phone box is waiting for its answer, the digits said are kept (nothing is filled yet, nothing is sent to Claude, and there is no clock: it waits as long as it takes) until there are enough: 13 for an ID, 10 for a tax or phone number, 11 for a phone number given as 27 …. Then the whole number is read as one line. A number can start while another box is being asked about ("my work number is 011" names its box, and the screen goes to it). The first piece may carry only filler words with it ("let me see", "um", "it's", "one moment"), so nothing else in the line is lost; once digits are held, any short line with three or more digits goes into them ("let me just check that out quick please 312 wait a minute" adds 312). A partial number from any other route (this computer's reading, the quick fill, Claude) is held too, never filled. Other talk is read as usual and the held digits stay. The conversation box shows each piece as it is kept ("holding 011 312 (6 of 10 digits so far)"), and the box on the form says how many are still to come. Saying it is wrong, "start again" or "let me give that again" drops what was kept; a whole number said again from the start replaces it; "add an 8 to the end" and the other spoken edits work on the held digits. Moving to another box ends it. A number said whole in one line, or one that comes with a question ("is that correct?"), is read as before.
- **Claude saying nothing keeps what was heard.** A clear answer filled at once is taken back only when Claude fills that box with something different (or puts the same number in another box); if Claude has nothing to say about it, it stays, amber, to be confirmed. This is why a cell number used to vanish.
- **Letting Claude see private numbers (Settings, off by default).** Normally an ID, tax or phone number or an email address is replaced by a marker such as [N1] before a line is sent, and the page fills the box itself. The switch "Let Claude see ID, tax and phone numbers and email addresses" sends them as they are, so Claude can fill, check and correct them (the instructions to Claude change to say so, and the card says "Claude sees ID, tax and phone numbers" while it is on). Turn it on only when the Anthropic account behind the key is covered by the agreement the firm needs for client details. Code: `setShare` in `js/voice-ai.js`, the switch in `js/ui.js`.
- **Editing a number by speaking.** With a phone, ID or tax number held in its box, "add an 8 to the end", "put a zero in front", "the last digit is 8", "it ends in 98", "change the last digit to 5" and "remove the last digit" change that number on this computer (the digits are never sent to Claude). The box goes amber again for the client to agree to.
- **Saying a value is not agreeing to it.** When a clear answer is filled at once, Claude's check of the same line can no longer confirm that box ("it's 082 454 1751" used to come back green); the client agrees in a later line.
- **"Scratch the first name and the surname"** (also clear, delete, remove, erase, wipe, forget, empty) empties the boxes named, with no call to Claude; "scratch that" brings the last one back.
- **Spelling a name.** A speech model gets an unusual surname wrong when it is said as a word (it heard "Kafentzis" as "Cafensus"). Spell it instead: "K A F E N T Z I S", "K-A-F-E-N-T-Z-I-S", "Kay, A, F, E, N, T, Zed, I, S" or "K for Kilo, A for Alpha, F for Foxtrot" are joined into one word ("Kafentzis") before the line is read. At least four letters in a row (three with "for"); "J. P. van der Merwe" and "plan A or B" are left alone. Two names spelled in one breath run together, so spell one at a time.
- **Chrome speech recognition**: instant and good for trying things, but Google hears the audio, so it is for practice talk only, never a client. No banner is shown while it listens (the Listen button says it is on); only a problem, such as the microphone being refused, is shown. A page opened straight from a file makes Chrome ask for the microphone again and again, because it cannot remember a choice for a file. **Start Fact Find.bat** (next to index.html) fixes that: it runs a small server on this computer only (FactFindServer.ps1, built into Windows, nothing installed) and opens the Fact Find at http://localhost:8765/, where Chrome remembers "Allow". The settings and the API key are kept per address, so they are entered once more at the new address. Close the small minimised window it leaves open to stop it. Chrome ends it after a long silence and the page starts it again. If Chrome's recognition freezes (the microphone hears speech for about 12 seconds and no text comes back, with no error), the page lets it go, starts a new one and says so; the level bar next to Listen now moves with the microphone for Chrome too, so a silent microphone is easy to tell from a frozen recognition. A recognition that never starts is started again after 5 seconds, and one that ends at once waits 1.5 seconds before the next so it cannot loop. An "aborted" error from Chrome that is not followed by an end (it never said it had stopped) restarts the listening after 1.5 seconds; our own stop does not. Each line also carries what else Chrome thought it heard and how sure it was (`speech: { sure, also }` in the saved log). Everything the microphone side does (listening on, speech heard, speech ended, errors, restarts) is written into the saved log as "(listening) ..." lines, and the ones that matter (restarts, errors) also show in the conversation box, so a silent gap can be explained.
- **OpenAI transcription (for names and addresses).** A third speech source, chosen in Settings (Speech source: OpenAI transcription, then paste an OpenAI API key; the model is gpt-4o-transcribe by default, gpt-4o-mini-transcribe is cheaper, whisper-1 is the older one; an "Open the OpenAI console" link under the key opens the page where the key is made). The page cuts the microphone into the same phrases as the other engines and sends each one to OpenAI's transcription service, which is far better than Chrome's recogniser on a name like Kafentzis. It is also given a list of the names and terms likely to be said (the adviser, the client and spouse names already on the form, Wealth Revolution, Pty, annuity, the product names and so on) so it spells them as they are filled in; Sitamah's pre-fill therefore helps the hearing. The hint list is built from names and product words (a name containing digits or an @ is left out, and no email address, phone number, ID number or amount is read from the form), plus the words of the line just before, to help with context; those words were already in the sound sent. Everything that is said does go to OpenAI as sound, ID numbers and email addresses included. It goes to OpenAI, not to Anthropic and not to Google: use it where the client has agreed, and keep the Chrome source for practice talk. It costs a few cents for a whole meeting. Like the other sources it shows its state under the top bar and in the listening log, and a bad key, no credit or no network is reported there in words instead of silence.
- **Hearing the client on a Teams call (OpenAI source).** Settings has "Hear the client on a Teams call": set it to Yes and pressing **Listen** opens Chrome's share window (asked for first, while the click still counts). Choose **Entire screen** and tick **Also share system audio** for the Teams app, or choose the Teams tab and tick **Also share tab audio** for Teams in Chrome. The page then listens to two sources at once: the microphone is the adviser (`who: me`) and the call's sound is the client (`who: client`), each cut into lines and sent to OpenAI on its own, so the page knows who said what instead of guessing (a client's "yes" confirms a read-back, the adviser's read-back does not). Only the sound is taken: the picture is thrown away, nothing is recorded, and the **Client Sound** bar under the top bar (next to **Paul Sound**, the microphone) shows the call's sound arriving. There is no status sentence while listening with Chrome or OpenAI: the Listen button and the two labelled bars say it is on, and only a problem is written out. If the share window is closed, or the share has no sound, the page says so in words and carries on with the microphone alone, with no speaker labels. If sharing is stopped in Chrome, or nothing is heard from the call for 30 seconds, it says that too. **Wear a headset**: with loudspeakers the microphone hears the client as well, and a microphone line that repeats a client line from the last ten seconds is dropped (`echo dropped` in the log), which is a safety net, not a cure. The call's sound goes to OpenAI with the adviser's, so the same care applies. The OpenAI source now also writes `listening on`, `line lost`, `slow answer from OpenAI` and `listening stopped` into the saved log, so a silent gap can be explained. Code: `openCall`, `tap` and `openaiEngine` in `js/listen.js`; tests in `tests/listen.test.js`.
- **Type of quote.** Personal risk quotes has a **Type of quote** dropdown (New Business, Servicing or N/A) after the FNA quote box, after the Shortfall quote box and as a column of every Reduced quote. Spoken answers are understood as "new business", "servicing", "N/A" or "not applicable"; "new business or servicing?" is read as a question, not an answer; a question that names the quote ("what type of quote is the shortfall?") points at that quote's box, and a plain "type of quote" takes the first one still empty. The printed PDF is one page longer with these boxes (the last page is the Income protection block).
- **Dropdowns open by themselves while the voice mode waits on them.** When the form points at a dropdown, or at a box with a list of choices such as Insurer, a list of the choices opens right under it, with the current choice marked. It scrolls when it is long, a click on a choice fills the box exactly like a change by hand, and it closes as soon as the box is filled (by voice or by hand), the form moves to another box, or Voice is turned off. A click anywhere else sends it away, and it stays away for that box. It is a list the page draws itself, because a web page cannot open the browser's own dropdown without a click. It is drawn from the same moment as the red-amber glow (`vmMarkWait` in `js/ui.js`), so it is open exactly while the box glows; it also works for the dropdown cells of list rows, such as the reduced quotes.
- **Meeting timer.** A **Meeting 0:00:00** button in the top bar times the whole meeting. It starts by itself the first time **Listen** is pressed on a new Fact Find (or click it to start); click it again to pause (for a break) and click to carry on. While it is paused a small **Reset** button appears. It counts from the computer's clock, so nothing is lost when the page is busy, and it is kept in the browser, so a reload or a crash does not lose it (a timer left running with the page closed for more than half an hour is taken as stopped when the page was last seen). **New** sets it back to zero and asks first when the meeting has run for a minute or more. The time is written into the saved voice log (`meeting: { seconds, running, text }`). Code: `js/timer.js`, tested in `tests/timer.test.js`; the button is in `index.html` and `js/ui.js`.
- **Chrome's five-minute cut-off is renewed at a pause.** Chrome's speech service ends every listening after about five minutes with a "network" error, and what was being said at that moment was lost. After four minutes the page now waits for the first pause in the talk (nothing heard for 1.5 seconds), asks Chrome to hand back what it has, and starts a fresh listening straight away, so the cut-off falls where nobody is speaking. If Chrome never confirms it stopped, the page lets it go and starts again after 3 seconds. It shows in the saved log as "listening renewed" and is not shown as a problem. It does not apply to the OpenAI source, which has no such limit.
- **Online copy (no launcher).** `.github/workflows/pages.yml` puts the app folder online at https://pavlos1972.github.io/Fill_In-Form_Project/ whenever `main` changes (set once: GitHub, Settings, Pages, Source: GitHub Actions). Chrome remembers the microphone for that address, so there is no launcher and no prompt each time, and a fix pushed to `main` is live a minute later with nothing to copy to OneDrive (the workflow stamps the scripts' `?v=` tags with the commit, so Chrome fetches the new ones). It is only the page: client details and the API key stay in the browser of whoever uses it and in the files they save, so Sitamah opens the same address on her own computer, fills in what is known, saves the client file into the client's OneDrive folder, and it is opened from there with Open. The settings and key are kept per address, so they are entered once at the new address. Because every page of the account shares that address's browser storage (a draft of the client is kept there), do not publish other pages under the same account name that are not trusted.

The microphone only hears this computer's microphone, so with a headset on a Teams call the client's voice never reaches it (the 27 October meeting: 274 lines, every one of them the adviser). Hearing the client needs the call's sound, which the next bullet adds for the OpenAI speech source. Code: `js/listen.js` (the sound cutting and the three sources, tested in `tests/listen.test.js`); the button and the line are in `js/ui.js`.

## What Claude costs (the meter in the top bar)

With an Anthropic key set, the top bar shows **Claude: US$0.0123 this fact find** and, once a credit balance has been typed in Settings, **about US$19.88 left**. Every answer from Claude says how many tokens it used (and how many web searches); `FF.lookup.costOf` in `js/lookup.js` prices them from the published prices (`PRICES`, checked October 2026: update them there when Anthropic changes them; a model that is not listed is priced as the dearest, so the meter never reads low) and the total is kept with the fact find: it is saved in the `.json` file as `claude` (never in the PDF), comes back when the file is reopened, starts at nothing for **New**, and is kept when the form is cleared by voice. Hover for the number of requests and tokens, click to open Settings.

"Left" is the balance typed under **Credit left in the Anthropic Console (US$)** in Settings, less what the page has counted since it was typed. It is an estimate: the page cannot know about anything else paid from the same credit, and Anthropic has no way to read a prepaid balance (its Usage and Cost API needs an organisation's Admin key, and does not exist for an individual account). Typing the Console's figure in again starts the count again; saving Settings without changing the box leaves it running. The top bar turns amber under US$5 and red under US$1, with a message once for each. The usage is billed to the Anthropic Console account that owns the key, not to a Claude subscription.

## Company look-up and the client's public profile

**What the company does.** When an employer or a business name is entered, typed or said, the page asks Claude, with web search, what the company does and fills "What does the company do?" or "What does the business do?" by itself (green flash in voice mode, shown as "heard"). It is only done when that box is empty or still holds the previous automatic answer: words you typed there are never replaced, and a changed name replaces the old description. If the company cannot be identified a short note appears and the box stays empty. The "Look up online" button still does it on demand. Only the company name is sent.

**Public profile (LinkedIn).** The "Public profile (LinkedIn)" box at the bottom of Client info is filled in when the "Search LinkedIn" button is tapped. (It used to start by itself, a few seconds after the last of the name, employer and occupation was entered, and again at every change to them; with Opus and up to four web searches each time, testing used up the credit, so it is off: `FF.autoProfile` in `js/ui.js` turns it back on.) Claude searches the public web, LinkedIn in particular, for the best match and writes who they work for and what they do, starting "Likely match:" when the name and the employer or occupation agree, or "Possible match, check it:" when only some of it does, with a source address (amber "verify"). If nobody plausible turns up the box says so. Searching again replaces the text unless you have written in the box yourself. Only the client's name, employer (or business) and occupation are sent, never an ID, phone number or email. It is a pointer to check with the client, not a fact, and it is left off the PDF (`nopdf` in `schema.js`). LinkedIn itself cannot be logged into or scraped, so only what search engines show publicly is available, and many people have no public profile.

Both need the Anthropic key: click **Settings** and paste a key (from console.anthropic.com). Web search must be switched on for the Anthropic account. The key is stored only in this browser. On the iPad practice page there is no key and no web search: what the company does comes from Claude's own knowledge (shown as "verify"; unknown companies stay empty) and the profile search does not run (the box says so).

## Voice mode

The **Voice** button in the black bar switches voice mode on and off. Nothing else appears on screen. The form itself shows where the conversation is:

**Speed.** Each line goes to Claude, which is most of the wait once the words are known. The request is about 7 000 tokens of instructions and form boxes that never change during a conversation, so with a key it is sent with the unchanging part marked for Anthropic to remember for a few minutes (prompt caching, `cachedRequest` in `js/voice-ai.js`); only the last few lines, the position and what is filled are new each time. The request also asks for low effort (`output_config.effort`) and, where the model allows it, no thinking at all (Haiku 5.5 `disabled`, Sonnet 5.5 `between_tools`; Opus 5.5 cannot switch it off; a model that refuses the setting is asked again without it): left to itself the model thinks before every answer, which takes seconds and is billed as output. The remembered part is kept for an hour, and is written when voice mode is switched on (`VAI.warm`, a request with `max_tokens: 0` and the same model, effort and thinking as a real line), so the first line is not slowed by it.

**Which model does the web look-ups, and what they cost.** Settings has two models. "Look-up model (web search)" is used only for the company description and the LinkedIn search; each reads several web pages (up to 3 or 4 searches, at US$0.01 each plus the pages read as input), so it is the dearest thing the form does. It defaults to Sonnet 5.5 (half the price of Opus 5.5 per token; Haiku 5.5 is a small fraction); a look-up model saved by the earlier version, which defaulted to Opus, is replaced once. The company description still runs by itself when an employer or business name is entered. The voice model reads the conversation (below) and costs a cent or so a line. The running cost of the fact find is in the top bar, and the Anthropic Console has the real figure by model.

**Which model reads the conversation.** Settings offers Sonnet 5.5 (marked recommended and the default), Opus 5.5 and Haiku 5.5. The first recorded conversations ran on Haiku, which invented actions, let its own reasoning leak into the reply and confirmed what had only been read back; Sonnet is the default for that reason. A voice model saved by an earlier version is replaced once by the default (`voiceSet` in the saved settings); a choice made after that is kept.

**Fill at once, then Claude checks** (OFF by default since the "my surname is spelled" mistake; a switch in Settings, and the page then checks with Claude as described here). Waiting for Claude takes seconds, so a clear answer (a name, a yes, a number: what the page's own reading, `V.plan`, understands with confidence, with no more than four fills or asks in a line of at most 16 words and nothing it is "tentative" about) goes into the form at once, as "heard", and Claude reads the line as well. `VAI.reconcile` compares the two: if Claude fills the same boxes the same way nothing changes and anything more it heard is added; if it reads the line differently, the boxes the page filled are put back (unless they have been changed since, `vmTakeBack`) and Claude's reading is used. While an earlier line is still waiting for Claude to be read, a later line is not filled at once (its place on the form depends on that line), and a line the page is not sure of waits for Claude as before. The strip under the top bar says which happened and how long each step took: "filled in 0.1 s, Claude checked it in 2.5 s", or "form updated in 10.4 s (waited 6.0 s for the line before, Claude 4.1 s)". The **Voice model** in Settings matters most: Claude Haiku 5.5 answers faster than Sonnet and costs a twentieth as much (US$0.10 per million input tokens against $2; cache reads $0.01 against $0.10; output $0.50 against $10). Usage is billed to the Anthropic Console account the key belongs to (API credits), not to a Claude subscription. The line under the top bar shows, for each line, how long the speech was, how long reading it took and how long the form then took, so a slow step can be found.

- The **section heading glows red-amber** when a topic is opened ("let's talk about your occupation").
- The **label and box glow red-amber** when a question points at them ("what is your occupation?"). The cursor is put in the box.
- When an answer comes, it goes in and the box **glows green** for a moment. If the box already held a value and the client agrees, it is confirmed (green); if the client disagrees, the box stays red-amber until the correction is heard, then it is replaced (green).
- "Scratch that" undoes the last change. "Clear that" empties only the box being talked about.
- "Remove everything" ("all my details are wrong, please remove them all", "wipe the entire form"): a window over the form asks "Delete all data from the entire form?". Nothing is cleared until "yes" is said or the button tapped; "no" (or the other button) keeps everything. Repeating the request is not a yes. After a yes, "undo" brings the whole form back, and a file you already saved is never touched. If the same line also stated facts, they are put in after the answer.
- With voice off, nothing is listened to and typing works as always.

**Natural, fluid talk.** It does not matter who says what. A line is split into parts and worked through in order, and a fact counts whoever says it: "your first name is John and your second name? Oh my second name is Peters" fills First name, moves to Surname and fills it. The words around an answer ("my surname is", "it's") are never typed into a box, and a sentence that stops before the answer makes the box glow and keep listening.

**Where the understanding happens.** Claude reads every line that is heard (`js/voice-ai.js`, model chosen in Settings). It works out what was asked, what was stated, what was corrected ("oh no, I made a mistake, it's ...": only the last version counts) and what the client does not have ("I don't have a telephone at home" ticks "No home number"), and which box each fact belongs in, then returns a list of things to do in order. The wording does not have to match anything: there are no code words and no list of phrases. This computer's own reading (`js/voice.js`) is used only for a bare command word ("scratch that", "next section"), for a one or two word answer, while notes are being taken, and as the fallback when Claude cannot be reached. Every line goes to Claude, however short (a one-word line such as "Kafentzis" is sent with the lines before it and the box being asked about), and the instructions tell Claude that speech comes in fragments: a line that announces something and stops ("my surname is spelled") holds no value yet and Claude waits, and the next line then fills the box. Claude saying "wait" also takes back anything the quick fill guessed for that line.

**One line, several things.** A line can move to another section and carry on there: "I'd like to talk about your investment planning. Do you have a tax free? Yes, an Old Mutual one, R3400 a month" opens Investments and adds the row (Tax-free savings, Old Mutual, R 3 400.00 a month). Claude is always sent the boxes, drop-down choices and lists of the whole form, not only the section that was open when the line began, so it can fill whatever section the line ends up in. As a safety net, if a long line only moves to another section and nothing is filled, the page asks Claude once more from inside that section for the rest of the line. The same goes for notes: "I'd like to make a couple of notes about the Old Mutual tax free. The client told me it is for his son's university fees when he turns 18" switches the notes on and writes the points in the section's Notes box ("- Old Mutual tax free: for his son's university fees", "- To be used when the son turns 18"). Claude writes the points; if it only switched the notes on, the page asks once more for them. A held-back number or email is never put in a note by Claude.

**What Claude never sees.** ID numbers, phone numbers, tax numbers and email addresses are never sent. Where the client says one, the text sent to Claude has a marker instead ("my cell is [N1]") plus its general shape ("10 digits, starts like a cell number"). Claude says which box gets which marker, including which of two numbers was the correction, and the real value is put in on this computer. A long number (9 digits or more) is always held back; a shorter one, such as a 7-digit tax number, is held back when it is said straight after a word like "tax number", "ID", "cell", "account" or "policy" (amounts such as "1 250 000" and years are not). The values already in private boxes are not sent either, only that a box is filled.

**Boxes and choices.** Dates are day/month/year. "I don't have a home number" ticks the "No home number" box. Nationality starts as South African and ID type as ID; "no, I'm Zimbabwean" and "I have a passport, A1234567" change them.

**Notes.** Every page has a notes box. "Let me take some notes" moves the glow to it and what is said next is added in point form until you move on; "note that down" adds the last remark. Where a page has several notes boxes (Medical, will & short-term) the one for the block under discussion is used ("notes on the will"). Notes stay on until they are ended, so everything said meanwhile is noted, not filled in. End them by saying "that's all for the notes" (also "let's end the notes", "I'm done with the notes", "stop the notes", "back to the form", or "let's move on", which also goes to the next question), by asking the next question or moving to another section (a question about another part of the form, such as "do you have gap cover?", or a fact that belongs in another box, such as "gap cover with Stratum", ends the notes by itself and goes to that box: this computer places the clear questions, Claude decides the rest), or by tapping the **Stop notes** button that shows beside the notes box while they are on. A note that landed in the wrong place is taken out by saying "undo" or by deleting its line in the box.

**Lists** (children, debts, existing policies, retirement funds, investments, partners and shareholders, and the quotes). A box in a list is "this row, this column". "Do you have any children?" makes the Add button glow and wait for a number; "two" adds two rows and moves to the first name. "Another child", "one more policy" add a row. "When was Thandi born?" and "the second child's name" go to the right row; a name already in a list points at that list. Claude can add several rows from one sentence ("two kids, Thandi born 22 August 2019 and Sipho in March 2021") and never duplicates a row already there.

Whatever listens (Chrome's recogniser, Whisper or Moonshine on this computer, or OpenAI transcription; another service could be added in `js/listen.js`) only needs to call `FF.app.voiceHear(text)`. An optional second argument, `'me'` or `'client'`, is only a hint. For testing, press and hold the Voice button to show a hidden test line.

File names are `Fact Find - dd.mm.yyyy - Name Surname`.
