# Wealth Revolution Fact Find

A Fact Find you fill in on screen and save as a PDF. Voice mode listens to the meeting and fills the form as it goes (see Voice mode below).

## Use it

Open `app/index.html` in Microsoft Edge or Google Chrome. There is nothing to install.

- **Fill in** each section from the list on the left, in this order: Client info, Occupation, Income & household, Debts, Children, Personal & group risk cover, Retirement funding, Investments, Medical, will & short-term, Health. Below them come three more pages: **Client priorities and quotes needed**, **BrightRock Risk Calculation** and **Admin**. The "Client priorities identified" heading that used to sit over them is gone, from the list, the screen and the PDF. The PDF and the voice "move on" follow the same order.
- **Save** keeps the client's answers as a `.json` file. **Open…** reopens one. Your work is also kept as a draft in the browser while it is not saved, so a closed tab does not lose it: when the page next opens, it asks whether to restore that draft or start a new Fact Find. Once the work is saved, the draft is removed from the browser, so the client's details do not stay behind in it (see "The browser draft goes once the file is saved" below).
- **Preview PDF** and **Save PDF** make the finished Fact Find in the Wealth Revolution layout. Lists such as debts and children continue onto extra pages as needed. Every Notes box (and the Comments and list-row notes) is printed in full in its own section, however long. The PDF's font has no shape for some symbols, so they print as plain text: "…" as three dots, a bullet as a dash, "→" as "->", "≥" as ">=", "≈" as "~"; curly quotes and long dashes are straightened, and accented letters are kept. Anything else without a shape (a tick, an emoji) prints as "?".
- **The PDF mirrors the form, page for page, always (a standing rule in `CLAUDE.md`).** A change to the form is made in the PDF in the same commit, and is not finished until `tests/pdf-mirror.test.js` and the browser check `tests/browser/audit-pdf.js` pass; the only things left off are the public profile (LinkedIn) and the Claude and OpenAI cost figures. A PDF saved earlier does not change by itself: save it again to bring it up to date. Every page, heading, box and note of the screen is in the PDF, under the same words, in the same order and the same shape: the notes under a heading (the LOA note, the RGAR note, the Direct Net Income note, the page introductions) are printed small in italics; each priority area prints its name and what the client decided, then the decision and comments (a short area's referral or request in the same row), then the summary, then its quotes; the Life quotes print as one table with **BrightRock: Fractured** and **Standard** side by side and the three rows FNA, Shortfall and Reduced; the **Standard quote details: what to quote** print as one table (Cover, Amount, Term / age, ACI, ABI, Waiting, Comments); Medical aid and Gap cover print side by side; BrightRock Risk Calculation starts with the line of what was asked for; Business assurance is one "Not applicable" line for a client who is not a business owner. The one box left off on purpose is the public profile (LinkedIn) summary. `tests/pdf-mirror.test.js` keeps it so: it replaces jsPDF with a recorder and checks that every box, heading and note of the schema is in what the PDF draws.
- **The top bar is in three groups.** On the left **Settings**, **New**, **Open…** and (since 5.2) **Folders…**; in the middle the meeting timer, **Voice** and **Listen**; on the right **Save**, **Save as…**, **Preview PDF** and **Save PDF**. The buttons have a row of their own under the title, so they stay in the same places on any screen width; on a narrow screen the three groups stack. The client's name and the save state (**Client Thandi Dlamini**, **no changes**) are centred in the title row, directly above the meeting, voice and listen buttons (on screens at least 1300 pixels wide; on a narrower one they sit beside the title). **Since 4.3 the keys and what they have cost are one small table** at the far right of the title row, one line for each service and aligned columns: **Anthropic**, the days its key has left (**30 days left**; amber at 14 days or fewer or with no expiry date, red once expired) and what it has cost this Fact Find (**US$0.052**, with **· about US$4.10 left** when a credit balance was typed in Settings); and **OpenAI**, the same. (It used to be four chips in a wrapped line: *Anthropic key: N days left*, *OpenAI key: N days left*, *Claude: US$… this fact find*, *OpenAI: US$… this fact find*.) A line shows only when its key is saved or it has cost something, the table only when a line does, and clicking a line opens Settings. Above it, always, sits the **Other forms ▾** menu holding the links **Risk Application ↗** and **Investment Application ↗**, which open those pages in another tab (see "Risk Application" and "Investment Application" below); **since 5.3 they open already filled with the client on this Fact Find screen** (see Release 5.3). On a narrower screen it wraps onto a line of its own, still at the right; on a phone-width screen it takes a row of its own.
- **The Shortfall quote needs a group.** When **Group risk through employer** (Personal & group risk cover) is **No** or **Not sure**, the Shortfall quote row of the Life area on Client priorities and quotes needed (both the BrightRock Fractured and the Standard shortfall quote) does not show its boxes. It says it once across the two, in the words "No Group exists for a Shortfall Quote to be compiled" (with where to change it), on the screen and in the PDF, and the voice mode skips those boxes. Nothing is deleted: set it back to Yes, or leave it unanswered, and the boxes return with what was in them.
- **Personal risk cover and group risk cover are on one page, each with its own heading.** **Personal & group risk cover** starts with **Has personal risk cover**, then **Joint life with spouse**, then **Existing personal risk policies**. Under **Group risk cover** comes **Group risk through employer** and **Existing group risk policies** (the same columns as the personal ones: Provider / administrator, Spend, Broker on record, Notes, with a total spend; no joint life). When there is no cover the policies list (and Joint life) stay on screen greyed out under the message "No personal risk cover is in place, so there are no policies to record" (for group: "No group risk cover is in place ..."), the same way the Spouse block explains itself; the voice mode skips them, and the PDF leaves them out. Answers still empty count as "not asked yet", so nothing is greyed until you answer. Voice knows the group list too ("a group policy with Discovery, R1 450 a month").
- **Retirement funding has a page of its own, with two lists.** **Retirement annuities** (provider, current or paid-up, value, monthly contribution, Broker on record) and **Pension and provident funds** (the same, with a Type of Pension fund or Provident fund) are separate lists (`ret_ra`, `ret_pp`), each with its own totals, under one Notes box. A Fact Find saved earlier with the single list opens with each fund in the list of its kind (a fund with no type is taken as a retirement annuity). Voice knows both ("I have a retirement annuity with Allan Gray", "a pension fund with Old Mutual").
- **Client priorities and quotes needed replaces the Advisor pages, and the Advisor name box is gone.** The advisor pages Personal risk quotes, Retirement & investment quotes, Referrals & documents and Business assurance are one page, **Client priorities**, with an area for each priority in the order **Personal Risk Covers (the Life area: life cover, disability, dread disease, income protection), RA, RGAR, Invest, EGAR, Medical Aid, Will, Short Term**, and Business assurance last. Every box and list of those pages is still there under the same ids, so earlier saved files open as before; the old notes boxes of those pages are now the one Notes box at the bottom (what was written in them is moved there, under its old heading, when an older file is opened). **BrightRock Risk Calculation** stays a page of its own, always in the list after Client priorities and quotes needed, whether or not the client has cover now or Life is a priority. **Documents to request** is removed. The PDF header still reads "Advisor Paul Kafentzis" (fixed text, `schema.advisor`).
- **Each priority area has a decision, a comments box and a summary.** The **decision** is Priority: client agrees, or **Not a priority** (empty until discussed; version 3.4: the one "no" is plain *Not a priority*, it used to read *Not a priority: client not interested*, so the reason is always written in the Comments, and an older file opens with the new words); for **Life, RA and Invest** there are two more choices in the middle, **Client not sure, needs to think about it** and **Client needs to check their budget**, which turn the area **yellow** and, unlike "not a priority", leave its quote boxes open (the PDF colours them the same way; by voice "let me think about it", "I need to speak to my wife first" fill the first and "I need to check my budget", "let me see what I can afford" the second). The **comments** box holds the reason or your instructions ("can't afford it now, revisit in six months"). A priority turns the whole area **green**; not a priority turns it **red**, and its quote and referral boxes grey out (the decision and comments boxes stay live, and the quotes drop out of the voice reading and the PDF). The quotes and referrals that belong to an area sit inside it: Life has the FNA, Shortfall and Reduced quotes in two kinds (BrightRock Fractured and Standard, see below), the standard quote details and Preferred monthly spend, with a button to the BrightRock Risk Calculation page; RA the RA quotes; RGAR the retirement goal analysis, with a note on what the report needs (the client's current or paid-up retirement annuities, pension and/or provident fund(s), and proof of income where there is a pension and/or provident fund; also printed in the PDF); Invest the investment quotes; EGAR the education goal analysis; Medical Aid, Will and Short Term their referral (Valerie, Capital Legacy, Cameron). **Business assurance** (Buy & Sell, Contingent liability, Keyman) is one grey line, "Business assurance applies only when Business owner is set to Yes on the Occupation page" (with a button to Occupation), until the client is a business owner.
- **The decision follows what the client already has (version 3.4, `app/js/autoprio.js`).** Three small things, none of them ever green and none of them an approach to the client.
  - **Automatic "Not a priority", only where a fact makes the area untouchable or pointless.** The form fills an **empty** decision, writes the **comment** from the facts and sets the area's referral or request box to **Not required**; all three show the grey **preset** tag, and the red line under the area says *Set from the earlier pages. Change the decision if the client wants it.* instead of *The client said this is not a priority.* The five rules: **6. Medical Aid**: Medical aid (or hospital plan) with Broker on record = Through company (the gap cover answer makes no difference); **7. Will**: Has a will Yes, up to date Yes, Held with Other provider (the comment names the provider and the date), or Held with Capital Legacy with Paul K as the broker; **8. Short Term**: Has short-term insurance Yes with Broker on record = Through company; **5. EGAR**: Has children = No. Life, RA, RGAR and Invest are **never** automatic (group risk through the company is the reason for the Shortfall quote). **It goes again** (decision, comment, referral) if the fact changes and nobody touched them; a comment the adviser edited stays. **Once the adviser changes the decision of an area that the form set, the area is his** and the form never sets it again (`auto.skip` in the saved file). A decision already made is never touched. **An older file opens with the outcomes the facts give** in every empty decision.
  - **Suggestions: one line and a button, while the decision is empty** (inside the area, under the boxes; nothing is stored until the button is pressed): privately paid **medical aid with Other broker** (*the client may be open to Valerie taking it over*, button **Valerie to contact for medical aid**); **short-term with Other broker, or paid by the spouse** (*if the client is open to a quote, Cameron can be asked*, button **Cameron to contact client**); a **will up to date with Capital Legacy under another broker** (*we have a code with Capital Legacy and can take over as brokers*, button **Capital Legacy to contact client**); **all children at the Tertiary stage** and **no retirement funds recorded** (no RA, no pension or provident fund, no living annuity; the RGAR needs them), both with the button **Not a priority**. The button is for when the client has said yes: it sets **Priority: client agrees** (or Not a priority for the last two), the referral or request box and, when it is empty, the comment, all as the adviser's own answer. Short-term with the client's own broker who is happy is the adviser's decision: the form cannot know.
  - **Cover with another broker is an opportunity to quote, never a take-over done (3.4, worded and corrected in 3.6).** A referral for cover that is with another broker (medical aid or gap cover, a will with Capital Legacy under another broker, short-term) ends *(currently with another broker: an opportunity to quote; the client will decide)* in the point for Sitamah and in the Priorities to be Focused on box (for a "both" referral where only one cover is with another broker: *the gap cover is currently with another broker: ...*). **Only when the Broker on record box was answered**: a box still showing the starting "Other broker" (preset) says nothing, and Short-term "Handled by spouse" says nothing (no broker is known).
  - **A line in the Checks** when the monthly amounts asked for (RA quotes, investment quotes and reduced quotes whose Request says Request a quote) are more than the **Disposable income** (*the client may need to check their budget*; nothing when the Disposable income is not worked out). **Type of quote** starts as **New Business** (Has personal risk cover = No) or **Servicing** (a policy with Paul K as broker), preset, for a quote that is asked for; a new reduced quote row starts the same way.
  - Tests: `tests/autoprio.test.js` (each rule, the comment, the referral, going again, taking the area over, old files, the suggestions and their buttons, the take-over wording, the affordability line, the Type of quote start) and the browser check `tests/browser/t-autoprio.js`.
- **The summary beside each area is written by Claude, in points, from that area's own boxes only.** `app/js/summary.js` collects, for each area, only the boxes and lists listed for it in `schema.panels` (Life: personal and group risk, credit life on each debt, and three totals the page adds up itself, never Claude: the **total debt to be covered** (the debt balances), the **monthly debt repayments** and the **childcare cost per month** (totals over every debt, whoever pays it; the BrightRock calculation counts only the debts the client pays, see below); RA: the retirement annuities, pension and provident funds and RA quotes; RGAR: the totals saved and contributed across all retirement funds; **Business assurance: the policies the client already has** (buy & sell, contingent liability, keyman, with insurer, cover and premium), whether there are partners and business loans or surety, or "Not applicable" when the client is not a business owner; Invest: the investments; EGAR: the children's ages, stage and cost; Medical Aid, Will, Short Term: their own boxes), and Claude is told to say only what the client has there (no advice, nothing from another area, "No group risk" or "No will in place" when it is not there). One request covers every area whose points are missing or out of date, sent when the page is opened; **Refresh** on an area rewrites just that one. Private numbers are scrubbed from what is sent, names are not sent for the children, and the points are saved with the file. Until Claude has written them (or with no Anthropic key) the page shows plain points built from the same facts, marked "Plain points from the form"; points are never shown once their area has changed. The points also print in the PDF under each area's heading, with its decision.
- **Broker on record is the same box everywhere, and starts as Other broker.** The six lists that have a broker (Existing personal risk policies, Existing group risk policies, Retirement annuities, Pension and provident funds, Investments, Existing business assurance policies) and the four single boxes in **Medical, will & short-term** (Medical aid, Gap cover, Will, Short-term insurance; they used to be Medical aid broker, Gap cover broker, Will broker and Short-term broker) are all labelled **Broker on record**, with the same four choices: **Paul K, Other broker, Through company, None** (**since 5.3 the Short-term insurance box offers Cameron Maritz instead of Paul K**: Paul is not a short-term broker; the other boxes and lists keep Paul K). A new row of a list starts as **Other broker**; the broker box of Medical aid, Gap cover, Will and Short-term insurance starts as Other broker from the first screen of a new form, before the answer above it is given, shown as a default (it is not counted in the page's progress, like the other defaults, and voice can still fill it). When the answer above closes the boxes (Medical aid None, Gap cover No or, since 4.6, Medical aid None for the Gap cover boxes too, Has a will No, Has short-term insurance No or Handled by spouse) the default that was not touched goes, so nothing is left under a No, and it comes back if that answer is taken away or changed again; a broker that was chosen (typed, picked or said) is never changed, and it can be changed at any time, by hand or by voice ("Paul is the broker", "through the company", "no broker" is None, "another broker" is Other broker; for short-term, "Cameron" or "Maritz" is Cameron Maritz and "Paul" is not a choice). **A file that is opened gets the same defaults** (it may have been saved before they existed): every policy row that has something in it and no broker gets Other broker (a row with nothing in it stays empty, and a broker that is there is never changed), and the broker box of Medical aid, Gap cover, Will and Short-term gets it unless its answer closes it (an answer not given yet does not). A row that holds only the default broker counts as empty (not in the progress, does not make the Yes/No a Yes, and is dropped when it was only pointed at). A file saved with the older choices keeps the fact: "Privately paid, no broker" is None, "Privately paid, other broker" and "Cameron Maritz" are Other broker. The short-term referral to Cameron is a different box and is unchanged.
- **The eight priority areas are numbered 1 to 8** on Client priorities and quotes needed, in the order they are on the page, on screen and in the PDF: **1. Personal Risk Covers, 2. Retirement Planning, 3. RGAR, 4. Investment Planning, 5. EGAR, 6. Medical Aid, 7. Will, 8. Short Term**. Business assurance has no number (it is not a priority the client decides).
- **Priorities to be Focused on: the green priorities, and what is needed for each.** The first thing on **Client priorities and quotes needed** is a box with that heading (id `focus`, saved in the file and printed in the PDF). It is **empty until the client agrees to a priority**, and then fills as the areas turn green: **only the green areas (Priority: client agrees) are in it**, in number order, each with its heading ("1. Personal Risk Covers") and, **since 3.6, three short parts**: **Recommend** (one line: the quote or analysis asked for, or the referral), **Client** (one line: the client's response, from your comment for the area, "Agrees." when there is none) and **Come back with** (at most three short lines, only when something is still to come: the other quotes asked for, further referrals, a policy schedule still to get). Credit life and who sends which schedule stay with the action points for Sitamah. A line longer than about 200 characters is cut. A yellow, a red or an undecided area is never in it; a green area with nothing asked for yet says so. It is written like the action points: plain points at once (`focusFacts`, `focusLocal` and `focusText` in `app/js/actions.js`), then **Claude's** (`focusPoints` in `app/js/voice-ai.js`; Claude gives `recommend`, `client` and `back` for each area, the numbered headings and the three labels are always put in by the form) a moment after the last change while you are on the page, **and when you arrive on the page or open a file** (a file saved before the box existed, with a green priority, fills it by itself, with no Refresh), for as long as you have not typed in the box; after that it is left alone and **Refresh** (one tap) writes it again over what you typed. The status line under the box says which. With no Anthropic key the plain points stay. The box is not read or filled by voice.
- **Claude is always told today's date.** Every request to Claude carries `today` (the weekday and the date from the computer's clock, "Thursday 05/11/2026"): each voice line, the catch-up check, the file notes, the area summaries, the action points, the Priorities to be Focused on, and the company and public-profile look-ups (which start "Today's date is ..."). It is not the same as the form's **Date completed**, which Claude is told as `meeting_date` and which can be another day. Every instruction says to use it whenever the date matters ("next Thursday", an age, how long ago). In the voice request it sits in the part that changes with each line, so the part Anthropic remembers is not touched.
- **Existing business assurance is recorded on the Occupation page.** When **Business owner** is Yes, the business details have **Business assurance in place** (Yes / No / Not sure) and, unless it is No, a list of **Existing business assurance policies** (type, insurer, cover amount, premium, who pays it, broker, notes, with totals), also known to voice ("we have a keyman policy with Discovery, R1 million"). These feed the Business assurance summary on Client priorities.
- **Client priorities works by voice.** The decision and comments boxes are in the voice index like any other box: "Is life cover a priority for you?" opens the choice list under that box and glows, "yes, definitely" / "go ahead" fills Priority, "not now" / "not interested" / "I can't afford it" / "I already have that" fills Not a priority, and a reason said with it ("I can't afford it at the moment, revisit in six months") goes into that area's comments box in a few words. Claude is told how these boxes work; the answers need no new words from you.

## Listening (the microphone)

**Listen: Off / On** in the top bar turns the microphone into the words voice mode reads (it switches Voice on too). Each line that is heard goes through exactly the same path as a typed line. A line under the top bar shows what is going on and the last thing heard, with a small meter for how loud the microphone is.

Three speech sources, chosen in Settings:
- **On this computer** (the default). The sound never leaves the computer. A speech model is downloaded once (Moonshine Tiny roughly 50 to 100 MB, Whisper Tiny about 40 MB, the Base and Small ones more) from Hugging Face by way of the jsDelivr CDN, and kept in the browser (IndexedDB), so it needs the internet only the first time. The model is chosen in Settings (Speech model). **Moonshine Tiny is the default**: Whisper always reads a full 30 seconds of sound however short the line, which took 5 to 6 seconds a line on the laptop without a graphics chip, while Moonshine reads only as much sound as there is, so a short answer comes back in a fraction of that. If Moonshine cannot start (a file that is not published, say), Whisper Tiny is used instead and the line under the top bar says so. The model runs in a background worker (so the page and the microphone never freeze while a line is being read; the worker is built from text in `listen.js` because a page opened from a file cannot start a worker from another file, and it asks the page to keep the model's files). It uses the graphics chip when Chrome offers one that really works (WebGPU, checked for a graphics adapter first) and the processor otherwise (the line under the top bar says which, and why); a graphics chip that fails is dropped at once, remembered, and the processor takes over in a fresh worker. If a worker cannot be started at all, the model runs on the page itself. Each line is cut at a pause of about 0.6 s, first brought up to a normal level (a built-in laptop microphone is often very quiet, and the models make words up when they hear too little), the number of words it may write is limited to what the line's length allows, and what the model writes for plain noise ("[BLANK_AUDIO]", "Thank you.", or the same few words over and over) is dropped, with a note saying the line was only noise and how loud it was. The loudness meter is on a decibel scale, so quiet speech still moves it. After each line the strip shows how long the speech was, how long reading it took, and how long the form then took to update, so every step of the delay can be seen.
- **Why the processor is used.** When the line under the top bar says "on the processor", the part in brackets says what Chrome reported about the graphics chip (no WebGPU on the page, WebGPU but no graphics adapter, or an error), so a slow laptop can be diagnosed from one screenshot.
- **Live Client Conversation.** A text box in the right-hand pane, under Checks (it stays in view while the form scrolls; the version and tax tables sit small under the heading at the top left). It is there from the start and says to press Voice. Every line heard goes in, oldest at the top and the latest at the bottom, and the box stays on the latest line unless you scroll it back to read. Under each line it says what was done on the form because of it ("filled Surname", "took back Cell number", "pointed at ID number") and how long each step took. A line is labelled Planner or Client only when the source says who spoke (the hidden test line with "me:" or "client:"); one microphone cannot tell the two apart, and the client's side of a Teams call is not captured yet. The box shows the last 200 lines; the saved log keeps the whole conversation of the form (up to 5,000 lines); **since 5.3 it is also kept in this browser as it is heard (see Release 5.3), so a reload no longer loses it**. **Save log** (top right of the box) saves a file of every line: what was heard, what Claude was shown and answered (with the lines before it), what the form did, and the timings. Numbers and email addresses are hidden in it (as [number] and [email], and as [N1] in what Claude was shown) unless the Settings switch lets Claude see them. It is for finding a problem: send the file along with the report.
- **A read-back is a question.** "Your ID number is 7212095268086, is that correct?" or "can you confirm your tax number" never confirms the box, even when it already holds that number: it only points at it, and it turns green when the client says yes. The words decide, because Chrome's recognition writes no question marks: "I just want to confirm you said R150 000, correct?", "you said ... is that right" and a line of five words or more that ends in "correct" or "right" without opening with an agreement ("so your wife's cell number is ... correct") are read-backs too, and the instructions to Claude say the same. The same value said again for a box (Claude repeating it for another box, or the adviser echoing what the client just said, "950,000. Perfect.") is left alone: it never turns a box green. Only the client's yes does.
- **Undo, clear and "empty this box" need the words.** Claude's "undo" is only done when the line says so ("scratch that", "undo", "never mind"), and "clear" or "empty" only when it says clear, delete, remove, erase or scratch. In the first recorded conversation a stray "undo" removed the Occupation and a "clear" on "is definite errors" wiped a name. One box named in the line ("delete his name", "remove the occupation") is emptied with Claude's `empty` action (an empty value for a box means the same); "scratch that" brings it back. The page's own guess that notes begin ("in the notes", "scratch all notes") is no longer trusted: those lines go to Claude, which starts notes only when someone wants to take notes.
- **An email address said in pieces is waited for.** While a personal or work email box is waiting, or one is named ("my work email address is Paul k"), or the line before said an address is coming ("my personal email address is"), short pieces ("it's pool one", "dot kafentzis", "at gmail dot com", spelled letters "K A F E N T Z I S") are kept on this computer, never sent to Claude, until the address ends in a domain (.com, .co.za ...). It is then read as one line, so Claude only ever sees the marker [E1]. "At", "dot" and spelled letters are part of the address, never filler; spoken digits before the "at" are digits ("paul k one" is paulk1); spaces are taken out ("Paul k at life risk dot co dot za" is paulk@liferisk.co.za). A whole address said or spelled in one line is written plainly first, so it is hidden from Claude the same way. "Let's try that again" drops what was kept; a line that is not a piece of an address ends it, and when what was heard already looks like an address it is put in the box, amber, so nothing said is lost. Because Claude never sees an address it cannot correct a misheard name in it, so the page does: a word of the address that sounds like the first name or surname already on the form ("cafences" for Kafentzis) is written as the form has it, and the log line says so.
- **"The email address is incorrect" and "delete that" empty the email box, at once.** They used to be taken for pieces of an address and kept (the box was never emptied and Claude never saw them). Now a short line with a delete word (delete, remove, clear, erase, wipe) and "that", "it" or "the personal/work email address", or a line saying the named email address is incorrect or wrong, drops whatever pieces were kept and empties the box (the page does it itself, with no wait for Claude; "scratch that" brings the address back), and the next pieces start a new address. "Scratch that" and "never mind" are requests and never start an address. A line with more in it ("I will remove it from my mailing list later") is not taken as a request. A misheard surname that begins with an extra vowel ("offenses" for Kafentzis) is matched to the form as well. Lead-in words such as "addresses" are no longer kept in the address.
- **A misheard address is repaired, not just kept.** Chrome's recognition writes names, letters and domains badly ("heaul1.aafem" for paul1.kafentzis, "with life rest.co.za" for "at liferisk.co.za"), and Claude never sees an address, so the page repairs it with what it knows: a word of the address that is a letter or two off the first name or the surname on the form is written as the form has it (a name with something added, such as "paulk" or "pkafentzis", is left alone), a domain a letter or two off a common one (gmail.com, outlook.com, mweb.co.za ...) or the firm's own (liferisk.co.za) is written as that one, and "with" or "add" heard for "at" is read as "at" when a domain follows. What was changed is said in the conversation box and the box stays amber. The list is `EMAIL_DOMAINS` in `js/ui.js`.
- **"Not" or "no" among the pieces of an address no longer turns it into "None".** The email boxes have a "No personal email" tick, and the page's general reading of an answer took the word "not" in "paul one, not defenses, at gmail.com" as "I have no email address", so the pieces were held for ever (found in the 14:40 log). Addresses are now read with `vmEmailParse`, which only joins the words into an address. "I need to correct that" (and "fix that", "redo it") also drops a number or address being held.
- **What Chrome's recogniser also thought it heard goes to Claude.** Chrome returns up to three wordings for each line; the best one was used and the others thrown away. In the 26 October log Chrome gave "my surname is confesses" at 92% sure (the other wordings were "compensus" and "confessis") and "we are not the broken record" ("Brokers on record" was one of the others). Now up to two other wordings are sent with the line as `heard_alternatives` (numbers and addresses hidden as usual), and Claude's instructions say to use them, to spell a name as it already is on the form ("confesses", "offenses" for Kafentzis), and list the South African mishearings seen so far ("pt1 limited" for (Pty) Ltd, "chair holding", "University" for annuity, "text number"). Chrome's own confidence does not tell a right line from a wrong one (mean 0.89), and "Kafentzis" never appeared among its wordings in 50 minutes: for names, a speech service that can be given the names is the real fix.
- **Undo and clear are never done on a guess.** With Claude available, "scratch that" or "cancel that" goes to Claude, which sees the lines before it; and on a read-back ("... [a number] correct") nothing is undone or cleared, because Chrome heard "can you confirm that" as "cancel that" and the cell number was removed.
- **Pause** (top right of the Live Client Conversation box, while voice is on) stops anything said being read, for a remark to yourself or a side conversation: the lines still show in the box, marked "paused: not read". **Resume** reads again.
- **A number said slowly, in pieces, is waited for.** While an ID, tax or phone box is waiting for its answer, the digits said are kept (nothing is filled yet, nothing is sent to Claude, and there is no clock: it waits as long as it takes) until there are enough: 13 for an ID, 10 for a tax or phone number, 11 for a phone number given as 27 …. ## Voice mode

The **Voice** button in the black bar switches voice mode on and off. Nothing else appears on screen. The form itself shows where the conversation is:

**Speed.** Each line goes to Claude, which is most of the wait once the words are known. The request is about 7 000 tokens of instructions and form boxes that never change during a conversation, so with a key it is sent with the unchanging part marked for Anthropic to remember for a few minutes (prompt caching, `cachedRequest` in `js/voice-ai.js`); only the last few lines, the position and what is filled are new each time. The request also asks for low effort (`output_config.effort`) and, where the model allows it, no thinking at all (Haiku 5.5 `disabled`, Sonnet 5.5 `between_tools`; Opus 5.5 cannot switch it off; a model that refuses the setting is asked again without it): left to itself the model thinks before every answer, which takes seconds and is billed as output. The remembered part is kept for an hour, and is written when voice mode is switched on (`VAI.warm`, a request with `max_tokens: 0` and the same model, effort and thinking as a real line), so the first line is not slowed by it.

**Which model does the web look-ups, and what they cost.** Settings has two models. "Look-up model (web search)" is used only for the company description and the LinkedIn search; each reads several web pages (up to 3 or 4 searches, at US$0.01 each plus the pages read as input), so it is the dearest thing the form does. It defaults to Sonnet 5.5 (half the price of Opus 5.5 per token; Haiku 5.5 is a small fraction); a look-up model saved by the earlier version, which defaulted to Opus, is replaced once. The company description still runs by itself when an employer or business name is entered. The voice model reads the conversation (below) and costs a cent or so a line. The running cost of the fact find is in the top bar, and the Anthropic Console has the real figure by model.

**Which model reads the conversation.** Settings offers Sonnet 5.5 (marked recommended and the default), Opus 5.5 and Haiku 5.5. The first recorded conversations ran on Haiku, which invented actions, let its own reasoning leak into the reply and confirmed what had only been read back; Sonnet is the default for that reason. A voice model saved by an earlier version is replaced once by the default (`voiceSet` in the saved settings); a choice made after that is kept.

**Fill at once, then Claude checks** (OFF by default since the "my surname is spelled" mistake; a switch in Settings, and the page then checks with Claude as described here). Waiting for Claude takes seconds, so a clear answer (a name, a yes, a number: what the page's own reading, `V.plan`, understands with confidence, with no more than four fills or asks in a line of at most 16 words and nothing it is "tentative" about) goes into the form at once, as "heard", and Claude reads the line as well. `VAI.reconcile` compares the two: if Claude fills the same boxes the same way nothing changes and anything more it heard is added; if it reads the line differently, the boxes the page filled are put back (unless they have been changed since, `vmTakeBack`) and Claude's reading is used. While an earlier line is still waiting for Claude to be read, a later line is not filled at once (its place on the form depends on that line), and a line the page is not sure of waits for Claude as before. The strip under the top bar says which happened and how long each step took: "filled in 0.1 s, Claude checked it in 2.5 s", or "form updated in 10.4 s (waited 6.0 s for the line before, Claude 4.1 s)". The **Voice model** in Settings matters most: Claude Haiku 5.5 answers faster than Sonnet and costs a twentieth as much (US$0.10 per million input tokens against $2; cache reads $0.01 against $0.10; output $0.50 against $10). Usage is billed to the Anthropic Console account the key belongs to (API credits), not to a Claude subscription. The line under the top bar shows, for each line, how long the speech was, how long reading it took and how long the form then took, so a slow step can be found.

- The **section heading glows red-amber** when a topic is opened ("let's talk about your occupation").
- The **label and box glow red-amber** when a question points at them ("what is your occupation?"). The cursor is put in the box.
- When an answer comes, it goes in and the box **glows green** for a moment. If the box already held a value and the client agrees, it is confirmed (green); if the client disagrees, the box stays red-amber until the correction is heard, then it is replaced (green).
- "Scratch that" undoes the last change. "Clear that" empties only the box being talked about.
- "Remove everything" ("all my details are wrong, please remove them all", "wipe the entire form"): a window over the form asks "Delete all data from the entire form?". Nothing is cleared until "yes" is said or the button tapped; "no" (or the other button) keeps everything. Repeating the request is not a yes. After a yes, "undo" brings the whole form back, and a file you already saved is never touched. If the same line also stated facts, they are put in after the answer.
- With voice off, nothing is listened to and typing works as always.

**Natural, fluid talk.** It does not matter who says what. A line is split into parts and worked through in order, and a fact counts whoever says it: "your first name is John and your second name? Oh my second name is Peters" fills First name, moves to Surname and fills it. The words around an answer ("my surname is", "it's") are never typed into a box, and a sentence that stops before the answer makes the box glow and keep listening.

**Lines are read at the same time, not one by one.** Every line that Claude has to read is sent to it the moment it is heard, so a line is never waiting for the one before it to come back (up to four are read together). The answers are put on the form in the order the lines were said, because a correction, a "yes" after a read-back and "scratch that" all depend on that order. Meanwhile the highlight follows the conversation as it happens: when a question the page can place itself ("What is your take-home pay?") is heard, its box is marked and its drop-down list opens at once, without waiting for Claude. An earlier answer that comes back after that fills its own box in the background and leaves the highlight (and the cursor, and anything being typed in that box) where the conversation is now. A reply is used only if the form is still as Claude saw it where the reply touches it: if the box it fills, a list it adds a row to, or the box the talk was on has changed since (another line filled it, added a child, moved on), that line is read again with the form as it is, so nothing is overwritten and no child is added twice. A bare "yes" or "correct", a number or email being said in pieces, notes, "remove everything" and edits such as "add an 8 to the end" are always handled in order. The saved voice log records per line when it was read at once (`read_at_once`), whether it was read again (`read_again`), whether its box was marked when the question was heard (`pointed_when_heard`) and how long it took from heard to on the form (`on_form_ms`); the conversation box shows the same time under each line. The early highlight is only used inside the section that is open and at boxes that already exist (a question about a box in another section waits for Claude, so the screen does not jump there and back), and a call that has not come back after 3.5 seconds is raced by a second identical request: whichever answers first is used (an occasional call takes 6 to 9 seconds where most take 1 to 2). If Claude's reply holds a second, corrected answer ("Wait, correcting that..."), the last complete answer is used, and a box of a list that Claude names as a plain box ("debts.prov") is read as that list box, so an answer is no longer lost to a slip in the wording.

**Heard but not placed.** A fact that was said but never reached a box is not lost: it goes into a list, "Heard but not placed", at the top of the Live Client Conversation box (and is saved with the Fact Find file, so it is still there when the file is opened again). A fact gets there when Claude hears something about the client that no box or list column fits (a boat loan, a plan to emigrate), when Claude gives a value for a box that is not shown for this client (a spouse's name for a single client), when a value cannot be read as the answer for its box, when a volunteered fact would overwrite a value the client has already confirmed, and from the catch-up check below. Each item shows the words, why it was not placed and the section it belongs to, with buttons: **Put in <box>** (when a box and value are known; the box goes amber to verify), **Add to <list>** (a child or loan that has no row yet), **Add to notes** (the notes box of that section) and **Dismiss** (it is not offered again). The line it came from carries "not placed: ..." in the conversation box and the saved log, and the open items are listed in the log as `not_placed_open`. The same fact in other words ("Has gout" and "Health: client also has gout") is listed once. **A figure in an item must have been said** (release 3.1): an amount Claude put in that is not among the figures in the lines just heard is not kept.

**Catch-up check.** The conversation is read again against the form, to find answers that were said but never reached a box. **Every value it gives carries the exact words it was taken from, and a value whose words are not in the conversation is dropped** (release 3.1). It runs when the talk leaves a section (only that section's part of the conversation, after a short pause for the lines still being read), when Voice is switched off (the whole conversation, once), and whenever **Check for missed answers** in the conversation box is pressed. Only empty boxes are filled, in amber "to verify", and nothing moves on the screen; a box that has a value is never changed (a different value in the conversation is offered in "Heard but not placed" instead), a box that was cleared by hand, by voice or with "scratch that" is left empty, ID, tax, phone and email boxes are never filled by it, a person or loan that has no row is offered as "Add to ..." rather than added, and lines that were paused are not read. The check appears in the conversation box as a "Catch-up" line saying what it filled. Settings has a choice: at each section change and at the end (the default), only at the end, or only with the button. It uses the voice model chosen in Settings and costs one request per check. It is shown what is already in each section's notes and what is already in the list, so it does not report a noted fact as missing or repeat itself, and it never fills a row that is still empty (it offers the fact instead).

**Said in pieces, said loosely, said in a batch.** A partner's or shareholder's ID and cell number are kept here while they are said in pieces, like the client's own ("but if I look it up, it is 720926" ... "1258088"): nothing is sent to Claude until the number is complete, and then it is filled as one number. "Last updated" (the will) and "Date of marriage" take a year or "about two years ago" (filled as the year, to verify), and a year alone passes the checks. "I'm left with R90 000 after all my expenses" is not the expense figure: with the take-home pay on the form, the expenses are filled as take-home minus that amount. A figure the adviser reads out from the calculator ("according to the SARS calculator your direct net income is ...") is not taken as the client's take-home pay. "Okay, so that's the end of noting that" ends the notes and is not written into them; the rest of the line is read as usual. Saying that all the answers are verified ("everything is fine, all the answers are verified") turns every answer still waiting for the client, in every section, to agreed; "scratch that" puts them back, and the page does it only when those words were said. The conversation log now shows what the notes received ("noted: ...") and the value each catch-up fill wrote.

**Where the understanding happens.** Claude reads every line that is heard (`js/voice-ai.js`, model chosen in Settings). It works out what was asked, what was stated, what was corrected ("oh no, I made a mistake, it's ...": only the last version counts) and what the client does not have ("I don't have a telephone at home" ticks "No home number"), and which box each fact belongs in, then returns a list of things to do in order. The wording does not have to match anything: there are no code words and no list of phrases. This computer's own reading (`js/voice.js`) is used only for a bare command word ("scratch that", "next section"), for a one or two word answer, while notes are being taken, and as the fallback when Claude cannot be reached. Every line goes to Claude, however short (a one-word line such as "Kafentzis" is sent with the lines before it and the box being asked about), and the instructions tell Claude that speech comes in fragments: a line that announces something and stops ("my surname is spelled") holds no value yet and Claude waits, and the next line then fills the box. Claude saying "wait" also takes back anything the quick fill guessed for that line.

**One line, several things.** A line can move to another section and carry on there: "I'd like to talk about your investment planning. Do you have a tax free? Yes, an Old Mutual one, R3400 a month" opens Investments and adds the row (Tax-free savings, Old Mutual, R 3 400.00 a month). Claude is always sent the boxes, drop-down choices and lists of the whole form, not only the section that was open when the line began, so it can fill whatever section the line ends up in. As a safety net, if a long line only moves to another section and nothing is filled, the page asks Claude once more from inside that section for the rest of the line. The same goes for notes: "I'd like to make a couple of notes about the Old Mutual tax free. The client told me it is for his son's university fees when he turns 18" switches the notes on and writes the points in the section's Notes box ("- Old Mutual tax free: for his son's university fees", "- To be used when the son turns 18"). Claude writes the points; if it only switched the notes on, the page asks once more for them. A held-back number or email is never put in a note by Claude.

**What Claude never sees.** ID numbers, phone numbers, tax numbers and email addresses are never sent. Where the client says one, the text sent to Claude has a marker instead ("my cell is [N1]") plus its general shape ("10 digits, starts like a cell number"). Claude says which box gets which marker, including which of two numbers was the correction, and the real value is put in on this computer. A long number (9 digits or more) is always held back; a shorter one, such as a 7-digit tax number, is held back when it is said straight after a word like "tax number", "ID", "cell", "account" or "policy" (amounts such as "1 250 000" and years are not). The values already in private boxes are not sent either, only that a box is filled.

**Boxes and choices.** Dates are day/month/year. "I don't have a home number" ticks the "No home number" box. Nationality starts as South African and ID type as ID; "no, I'm Zimbabwean" and "I have a passport, A1234567" change them.

**Sections that are finished.** The adviser works through the sections one by one, so the page listens for the end of one: "that concludes the debt section", "that's the debts done", "we're done with income", "that's all for this section", or the client's "no" to "do you have any more debts?" / "anything else?". That is not small talk and it fills nothing: the section gets a tick in the list, the conversation log says "section finished", the catch-up check looks at it, and the page moves on to the next section (not when the same line goes somewhere else itself, such as "that concludes the debts, let's talk about the children"). "Scratch that" takes both back. Where a page holds two lists (personal and group policies, retirement annuities and pension funds) a "no more" finishes only that list, and where it holds several topics (medical aid, will and short-term insurance) "that's the will done" finishes only that part, not the page. The client's own "my debts are all done", a question, or "any other health issues?" (a box of its own) is not an end. Saying something more about a finished section ("oh wait, there is one more debt, a car loan with Wesbank") opens it again. Claude is told which sections are finished (`position.concluded`) and gives a `done` action; this computer's own reading (statements such as "that concludes ...", and a "no" straight after an "any more ...?") is used when Claude cannot be reached.

**Notes.** Every page has a notes box. "Let me take some notes" moves the glow to it and what is said next is added in point form until you move on; "note that down" adds the last remark. Where a page has several notes boxes (Medical, will & short-term) the one for the block under discussion is used ("notes on the will"). Notes stay on until they are ended, so everything said meanwhile is noted, not filled in. End them by saying "that's all for the notes" (also "let's end the notes", "I'm done with the notes", "I've noted that, I've completed those notes", "that's noted", "nothing more to note", "stop the notes", "back to the form", or "let's move on", which also goes to the next question; a line that mentions the notes, or a short wrap-up such as "that's everything for this one", is also given to Claude to decide, and the words that end the notes are never written in them; what is said before them in the same line is still noted), by asking the next question or moving to another section (a question about another part of the form, such as "do you have gap cover?", or a fact that belongs in another box, such as "gap cover with Stratum", ends the notes by itself and goes to that box: this computer places the clear questions, Claude decides the rest), or by tapping the **Stop notes** button that shows beside the notes box while they are on. A note that landed in the wrong place is taken out by saying "undo" or by deleting its line in the box.

**Lists** (children, debts, existing policies, retirement annuities, pension and provident funds, investments, business assurance policies, partners and shareholders, and the quotes). A box in a list is "this row, this column". "Do you have any children?" points at the Has children box (the Yes/No); "How many children do you have?" makes the Add button glow and wait for a number; "two" adds two rows and moves to the first name. "Another child", "one more policy" add a row. "When was Thandi born?" and "the second child's name" go to the right row; a name already in a list points at that list. Claude can add several rows from one sentence ("two kids, Thandi born 22 August 2019 and Sipho in March 2021") and never duplicates a row already there.

Whatever listens (Chrome's recogniser, Whisper or Moonshine on this computer, or OpenAI transcription; another service could be added in `js/listen.js`) only needs to call `FF.app.voiceHear(text)`. An optional second argument, `'me'` or `'client'`, is only a hint. For testing, press and hold the Voice button to show a hidden test line.

File names: **the Fact Find saved as a PDF and the saved Fact Find file (.json) have the same name, `Fact Find (Audio to Text) - dd.mm.yy - (Name Surname)`**, for example `Fact Find (Audio to Text) - 29.10.26 - (Sitamah Nathoo)` and then `.pdf` or `.json` (until 3 November 2026 the PDF said "Voice to Text" and the .json was `Fact Find - dd.mm.yyyy - Name Surname`): the date is the form's **Date completed**, written day.month.two-digit year, and the client's name and surname are in round brackets (`(New client)` until a name is entered). This is built in `store.pdfFileName()` (`store.baseFileName()` returns the same for the .json) and used by **Save**, **Save as…**, **Save PDF** and the fallback downloads; a file that is opened keeps its own name when it is saved again, so only the first save of a client's file and every PDF get the name above. Since 5.2 a first Save and every Save PDF go into the client's `2. Fact Find` folder under this name when the folder can be found (see Release 5.2); the name itself is unchanged. Do not change it without being asked.

## Not built yet
